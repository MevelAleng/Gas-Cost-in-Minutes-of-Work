# The Cost of a Gallon of Gas, in Minutes of Work

**By Meve Lako | Power BI capstone | 2006 to 2025**

## What this is
A two-page Power BI report that asks: **how many minutes of work does a gallon of gas cost, and has that changed?**

Instead of comparing prices, it divides the gas price by the average hourly wage in the same year. That shows how much work it takes to fill up, and it largely cancels out the effect of inflation.

## What's inside
- **Title page:** the question and a Start button.
- **Executive Overview:** *Is gas harder to afford than it used to be?* Gas took about 8.2 minutes of work in 2006 and about 5.5 minutes in 2025.
- **HR Overview:** *How does a stylist's pay hold up against gas costs?* Stylists earned about 63% of the average worker's wage in both 2006 and 2025, so they need roughly 8.8 minutes of work per gallon, against 5.5 for the average worker.

## Where the data comes from
- **Gas prices:** U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), weekly regular gasoline prices, averaged by year.
- **Wages:** U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS), Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, for all workers and for hairdressers, hairstylists and cosmetologists.

## Things to keep in mind
- Dollars are **not adjusted for inflation**, and wages are before tax.
- Wages are a May estimate, while gas is a full-year average.
- The stylist figures cover **employees only**. They include tips, but not self-employed or contract stylists, such as booth renters.
- 2006 has no "change from the prior year," because there is no earlier year in the data.

## How to use it
Open the file in Power BI Desktop. Use the year slider to narrow the charts, and hover over a point on the Executive line chart to see that year's gas price and wage. In Desktop, hold **Ctrl** and click to use the navigation buttons.
